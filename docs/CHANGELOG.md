## 2026-07-14 (test integrity: six fixture-drift guards were vacuous — cross-realm `notDeepEqual`)

- **fix(gas): the suite's core rule was being enforced by guards that could not fail.** Investigating whether Stage 6's cross-realm `deepEqual` finding affected the already-merged suites turned up a **worse variant of the same bug, in the opposite assertion** — and it inverts the assumption that `notDeepEqual` is the safe direction.
- **The trap.** `Code.gs` is evaluated in a `vm` context with its own intrinsics, so `LEAD_HEADERS` / `UNIFIED_LEAD_HEADERS` carry that context's `Array.prototype`, not the host's. `assert/strict`'s deep comparisons check prototypes with `===`. Therefore: `deepEqual(hostArray, vmArray)` **throws even when the contents match** (loud, self-correcting), but **`notDeepEqual(hostArray, vmArray)` PASSES even when the contents are IDENTICAL** (silent). The second is the dangerous one: it is exactly the tautology the suite's fixture rule exists to prevent.
- **Six guards were vacuous:** the `assert.notDeepEqual(MANGLED, UNIFIED_LEAD_HEADERS)` fixture-drift checks in `referrer-stats`, `cold-sweep`, `status-edit`, `on-sheet-edit`, and `manual-referral-link`, plus `pure-functions`' 5-element literal. Each is meant to prove the hand-typed fixture is not secretly a copy of the constant under test — **and each passed whether it was or not.** Proven by sabotage: a fixture replaced with a byte-for-byte copy of `UNIFIED_LEAD_HEADERS` sailed through the old form and fails the fixed one.
- **The asymmetry that made it invisible:** a fixture built by *transforming* a sandbox constant (`LEAD_HEADERS.slice(5).concat(...)`) stays in the **vm** realm and compares correctly; a fixture *typed by hand* in the test file is a **host** array and does not. Identical-looking assertions, opposite behavior — which is why the rotation-based guards worked and the hand-typed ones did not.
- **Coverage was degraded, not absent.** Every affected file also carries a per-column canonical-index guard (`assert.notEqual(colOf(...), UCOLS[key])`) which compares **numbers**, is realm-agnostic, and was doing the real work — it caught two genuine accidental collisions during Stages 1 and 3. The `notDeepEqual` line beside it was the broken one.
- **Fix:** all 13 header comparisons across the suite now lift the sandbox value with `Array.from(...)` before comparing — uniformly, including the rotation-based ones that happened to work, because they only worked by an accident nobody would remember. The trap is documented at the one place that creates the realm boundary, `tests/helpers/load-code.js`.
- **NO positive `deepEqual` in Stages 1-5 was affected**, and structurally none could be: a cross-realm positive `deepEqual` **fails loudly** rather than passing silently. The only two that compare vm-realm arrays (`leadTabConfigs`, `BOOKING_SLOTS` vs the frontend `SLOTS`) already used `Array.from` with a comment — whoever wrote them had hit this and handled it.
- **Test-only change. `Code.gs` is untouched, so no GAS deploy is required or implied.** 105/105 green.

## 2026-07-14 (unified-schema migration STAGE 3: `handleStatusEdit` — and the Stage-2 concurrency prediction was wrong)

- **refactor(gas): Stage 3 migrates `handleStatusEdit`,** the second of the file's two row-deleting functions. Same pattern: `handleStatusEditUnified` / `handleStatusEditLegacy` (verbatim) / dispatcher on `USE_UNIFIED_SCHEMA`, still **off**, so this merge is again a **behavioral no-op in production**. **With this stage, no unified path in the file deletes a lead row anywhere** — both deleters are migrated, and in both the deletion logic was *removed*, not ported.
- **The change: legacy treats a status as a PLACE, unified treats it as a VALUE.** Legacy's `'Cold'` means "copy this row to the Cold Leads tab and delete it from Active"; `'Client'` means "copy it to Clients"; `'Archive'` means "copy and delete". Unified does none of that, because **the human already wrote the cell** — that write is what fired the `onEdit` trigger. The row moves nowhere, nothing is appended, nothing is deleted, and all that remains is the Google Contacts side effect. Most of the function disappears, exactly as the plan said it would. The unified branch **deliberately ignores the `sheetName`/`editedCols` it is handed**, because those come from the not-yet-migrated `onSheetEdit` and are `LEAD_HEADERS`-shaped; feeding a legacy column map to a unified reader is the exact bug class this migration exists to end.
- **CORRECTION — Stage 2's stated fix for the sweep-vs-human-edit gap was wrong, and the correction is the important part of this stage.** Stage 2 predicted the gap would close once `handleStatusEdit` took the same lock. **A lock could never have closed it.** The human's `Status` write is performed by the **Sheets UI**, which takes no lock and cannot be made to; by the time the `onEdit` trigger runs, the cell is *already written*. Giving the handler a lock lets it **notice** a clobber, not **prevent** one.
- **What actually closed it: two mechanisms, both required.** (1) **The shared lock** — `updateReferrerStats`, `moveColdLeads`, and `handleStatusEdit` now all take the one process-wide `getScriptLock()`, so their critical sections genuinely contend and Contacts side effects can never be decided from a row a sweep is halfway through rewriting. (2) **A pre-write live re-read inside `sweepStaleLeadsToCold`** — the sweep re-reads each row's `Status` immediately before stamping `'Cold'` and backs off if it is no longer active, shrinking the clobber window from *the entire sweep* to the microseconds between that read and the write. **This is the part no lock could do**, and it is a (scoped-in, deliberate) touch to Stage 2's function. **The rule to carry forward: a lock only protects against writers that take it.** A race whose other party is a human in the Sheets UI must be handled by re-validating at the point of write.
- **On a status conflict, the handler acts on the LIVE value and does NOT auto-restore.** Contacts side effects are driven off the status read under the lock, never off the (possibly stale) edit event, so the Sheet and Google Contacts can never disagree. The conflict is logged loudly, and the cell is deliberately left alone: nothing can distinguish "a sweep stamped Cold over their Client" from "the human edited again a second later", so auto-restoring would silently revert a deliberate human edit — trading one rare bug for another rather than fixing one.
- **Tests: 75 → 87, all green.** New `scripts/gas/tests/status-edit.test.js`. Its centerpiece is the **shared-lock contention proof**, which is what this stage is actually for: a real stateful exclusive lock (not a stub that always says yes), with `getScriptLock()` returning **the same object** every call as GAS does, and the two functions driven **from inside each other's critical section** — a status edit arriving mid-sweep is REFUSED, a sweep arriving mid-edit is REFUSED, and running them sequentially never contends (so no path leaks the lock). Two independent locks would sail straight through and fail these. Plus: the **clobber guard** (mutating the cell between the sweep's snapshot and its write, proving the human's `Client` survives *and* that the genuinely-stale lead beside it still goes Cold); every unified status path asserting **zero appends, zero deletions**; the live-vs-event conflict; a refused lock skipping only Contacts; and **the legacy branch across all four status paths**, asserting it still appends-and-deletes exactly as production does and takes **no** lock.
- **Honest limits, same as Stages 1 and 2:** none of this proves Apps Script's `LockService` delivers real mutual exclusion — there is no concurrency in a Node harness. What is proven is what is ours to get wrong: that both functions reach for the **same** lock object, that a held lock refuses the other, that neither leaks it, and that the sweep re-verifies before it writes. Real mutual exclusion is Google's guarantee, observable only live.
- **Harness fix:** `FakeRange` had no `getValue()` — a real gap in the fake's fidelity (Apps Script ranges have it), exposed the moment the sweep's re-read became the first code to call it. Added.
- **Next: Stage 4, `onSheetEdit`** — deliberately taken **out of §3's table order** (which would put `sendMonthlyReferralSummaries` next). `onSheetEdit` is the **sole caller** of all three edit handlers, and until it is migrated `handleStatusEditUnified` is unreachable; migrating a caller alongside the handler it feeds is lower-risk than leaving them split across four stages. Also recorded: **`setCategoryTabStatus` is not a stage at all** — it is a §4 delete-outright and cannot go until the cutover removes `moveColdLeadsLegacy`, which still calls it.
- **Not deployed, and not live.** The `Leads` tab still does not exist. Touches `Code.gs`, so a deploy will eventually be needed — but there is deliberately nothing to ship for this stage, because the switch is off.

## 2026-07-14 (unified-schema migration STAGE 2: `moveColdLeads` — row deletion removed, not ported)

The **Contacts calls and the summary email are deliberately outside the lock** — the GAS script lock is process-wide, and holding it across a Contacts round-trip would stall every submission's referral credit.
- **Documented gap, not a silent one — and it is what Stage 3 exists to close:** the lock serializes **sweep vs sweep**, but **not sweep vs a human's `Status` edit**, because `handleStatusEdit` does not take the lock (it is not migrated yet). A human promoting a lead to `Client` mid-sweep can still be clobbered by a stale `Cold` write. Recorded in `backend-architecture.md` → *Known open defects* §5 and in the plan's Stage-3 notes. **A lock only excludes writers that take it**, and claiming otherwise would be worse than no lock.

The header fixture is hand-typed and mangled **differently from Stage 1's**, on purpose: two fixtures that scramble identically are one fixture.

Actual mutual exclusion is Google's guarantee and is only observable live. Same framing as Stage 1, deliberately.

Touches `Code.gs`, so a deploy will eventually be needed — but there is deliberately nothing to ship for this stage, because the switch is off.
